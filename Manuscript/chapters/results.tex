\section{Glaciated areas}

The degree day model developed within the framework of this thesis produces daily snow water equivalent maps according to Equation \ref{eq:swe_swe}. Since 
the SWE variable is set to zero only at the beginning of the simulation and is not reset thereafter, any snow remaining at the end of the season is carried 
over to the following year. Consequently, in high elevation alpine catchments where temperature do not allow complete snowmelt, pixels can keep accumulating 
snow for multiple years. In particular, grid points located above \SI{3500}{\meter} a.s.l. appear to experience continuous snow cover throughout the 
whole \num{72} years period between \num{1951} and \num{2023}, as illustrated in panel (a) of Figure \ref{fig:csc_vs_ele}. Moreover, a significant share 
of pixels above \SI{3000}{\meter} a.s.l. appear to experience all year round snow coverage at least once.
\begin{figure}[H]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{results/csc_vs_ele.png}
    \caption{(a) Number of years with snow cover all year round as a function of elevation. Each dot is a grid point of the simulated domain. (b) Distribution 
    of cells with at least one year of continuous snow cover as a function of elevation.}
    \label{fig:csc_vs_ele}
\end{figure}

Although this phenomenon is completely consistent with snow dynamics in the Alps, as it represents one of the main processes governing glacier ice 
formation, it requires appropriate handling to avoid incorrect estimates of seasonal snow cover dynamics and trends over the study area. Indeed, 
since grid points are treated independently, snow can accumulate indefinitely at higher elevations, leading to unrealistically deep snowpacks that persist 
throughout the simulation. A possible solution to this issue would be to develop a glacier module that simulates the transformation of snow into firn and 
ice, as well as glacier flow, thereby allowing mass to be transferred downward to milder thermal conditions.  However, this is beyond the scope of this 
thesis. The adopted solution was instead to exclude from SWE calculations those pixels that remained snow covered for at least half of the investigated 
period, i.e., \num{36} out of {72} years. These grid points are located on the most important massifs of the Italian Alps, as illustrated in panel (a) of 
Figure \ref{fig:covered_area_maps}, and roughly resemble the average glacier extent over the 1951--2023 period. The masked area accounts for approximately 
\SI{423}{\kilo\meter\squared}, representing \num{0.27}\% of the study area.


\begin{figure}[H]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{results/covered_area_maps.png}
    \caption{(a) Spatial distribution of grid points that were snow covered all year round at least once. Colours indicate the total number of years with 
    continuous snow cover. (b) Temporal evolution of the area snow covered all year round over the 1951--2023 period. A nearly linear decrease can be 
    observed over recent years, suggesting that meteorological conditions are becoming increasingly unfavorable for the persistence of the cryosphere.}
    \label{fig:covered_area_maps}
\end{figure}
