\chapter*{Summary}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{Summary}

\noindent
This thesis aims to reconstruct snow water equivalent (SWE) across the Italian Hydrological portion of the Greater Alpine Region 
(IH-GAR) using high-resolution temperature and precipitation data. To this end, a degree-day model was developed to simulate both 
snow accumulation and melt processes. In parallel, an observational snow depth (HS) dataset was assembled through a data 
rescue activity and its main numerical issues were subsequently addressed, to ensure the reliability of these HS series. This 
step proved essential for the optimization of model parameters, which was based on comparisons between simulated SWE and observed 
snow depth time series. The calibrated model was then used to generate daily SWE maps for the 1951--2023 period, thereby allowing 
for robust trend estimates. The results reveal a decline in annual peak SWE of \SI{-0.567}{\giga\meter\cubed} per decade over the 
1952--2023 hydrological record, corresponding to an average reduction of approximately 35--40\% in maximum annual SWE over a time 
span of just 72 years. Furthermore, the decline of snow water equivalent is not uniform across the study area, but varies markedly 
with elevation and throughout the snow cycle. In particular, the most pronounced negative SWE trends, accompanied by higher levels 
of statistical significance, were detected during spring months in the highlands and below \SI{1000}{\meter} a.s.l. almost all year 
round. These reductions in SWE are accompanied by a shortening of the snow season, mainly driven by earlier snowmelt during the 
spring months. As global warming continues, further loss are expected. A possible extension of this work would be to investigate 
future changes in SWE under different climate change scenarios. 

\bigskip
\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} Snow Water Equivalent (SWE), degree-day model, climate change, snow depth.
